# Law Firm Branding Checklist

Branding for a law firm is not about a clever logo. It is about being recognisable and trustworthy everywhere a client might meet you: the sign on the door, the website, the Google listing, the business card and the email signature. This checklist covers the parts that matter for a new or growing firm. Published by Legal Web Agency.

## 1. The name

| Check | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Allowed by your state bar | Trade names and partner-name rules differ by state |
| Easy to say and spell on the phone | People hear it before they read it |
| Domain available (ideally .com) | The website address should match the name |
| Not confusingly close to another local firm | Avoid confusion and disputes |
| Searchable | A generic name is hard to find in search results |

## 2. The logo

- Works in one colour and at small sizes (a browser tab, a profile photo).
- Readable wordmark; avoid gavels, scales and columns unless you can make them distinctive.
- Delivered as vector files (SVG or PDF) plus PNGs, owned by the firm.

## 3. Colours and fonts

- Two or three colours at most, with one accent colour reserved for buttons and calls to action.
- Text colours that pass contrast checks on white and on dark backgrounds.
- One heading font and one body font that are easy to read on a phone.

## 4. Voice

Decide how the firm sounds: plain and calm, warm and personal, or direct and energetic. Write it down in three or four sentences and use it for the website, emails and social posts. Whatever the voice, avoid claims your state's advertising rules restrict, such as "best" or "expert".

Inconsistent details confuse clients and make it harder for search engines and AI tools to connect the firm's listings.
